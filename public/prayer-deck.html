Still & Near

A deck of small invitations into prayer. Bring gratitude, grief, needs, and people to God. Begin where you actually are, not where you think you should be.

Enter and leave prayer

One physical cue. One honest moment.

Arriving

Make a small threshold. Put your phone face down, feel your feet, and soften your shoulders if that is comfortable. You may make the sign of the cross, hold a smooth stone, or simply open your hands.

Rest your hands palm-up on your knees. Notice that you do not have to earn God's attention.

Father, here I am. Holy Spirit, help me to pray.

Returning

Close without rushing. Name something you are leaving with God. Lower your hands gently or stand up slowly, then return to the next ordinary act of love.

Thank you for hearing me. Give me grace for the next faithful thing. Amen.

Postures are invitations, not requirements. If knees, neck or balance make a position uncomfortable, sit or lean against a wall instead.

One pause of thanks

Attach this to breakfast, lunch or turning off the light.

Notice. What was one real gift today? A person, a small mercy, fresh air, help, food, a moment of peace. Open your palms or place a hand on your chest if comfortable.

Father, thank you for this. Help me receive your goodness.

This remembers only that you paused today. Your words are not stored unless you explicitly save a prayer card below. No streaks, scores or guilt.

Pray the room

An unhurried practice of intercession.

Imagine sitting with Jesus or the Father in a quiet room. Invite the Holy Spirit to bring a person or circumstance to mind. A thought is an invitation to pray and discern, not proof that God has made a particular promise or revealed a hidden fact.

    Write your own lament

    Venting your feelings to God is allowed.

    You don't have to make your grief tidy. This is not a gratitude exercise disguised as lament. Psalm 13 offers a pattern; Psalm 88 shows that a prayer need not end happily.

    Your lament — yours to pray

    Nothing in this editor saves automatically. Only pressing “Save as a private prayer card” stores the composed prayer on this device. You may also just pray it aloud and clear the draft.

    Practice reflection

    A gentle evening return, not a daily exam.

    Remember your prayer practice. What was life-giving? What was difficult or distracted? What did you notice in your body, and what might you try tomorrow?

    Open the official Prayer Practice and companion guide for its exercises and reflection questions.

    Return here each evening if useful. This page cannot send scheduled push reminders by itself; your ChatGPT task list currently has no free reminder slots.

    Keep it real and repeatable

    A tiny cue–action plan

    After I put my feet on the floor in the morning, I will open my hands and pray one sentence. After lunch, I'll pause to give God thanks once. At bedtime, I'll reflect and release the day.

    Keep the prayer and the cue consistent, rather than aiming for a streak. If you miss a day, simply begin again.
