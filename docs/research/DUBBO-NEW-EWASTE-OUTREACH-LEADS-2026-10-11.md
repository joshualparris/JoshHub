## Executive decisions

1. **Start with independents:** Custom Computer Creations, Right Click Go, Inland Technology and Page Electronics were not found among the 21 messages. Custom has the best hardware-repair relevance; Inland is print-focused; Page is television-focused.
2. **Then ask asset-owning employers:** Westhaven, Fletcher, MAAS, Earlyrise and Inland Petroleum have local organisational footprints, but **neither actual retirement stock nor release authority is established**.
3. **Approach education carefully:** Independent schools may route decisions through local business managers. Catholic schools may need diocesan permission. NSW public schools have a dedicated eWaste contract via EDConnect; do not request that school employees hand over retired devices outside it.
4. **Do not conflate a contact email with an IT manager.** General/sales addresses are routing points only. Request referral to the person who owns and authorises equipment disposition.
5. **Do not approach Avance clients using information gained at work.** No employer records, customer contact lists or confidential device-disposal details are used here.

## How to contact without damaging relationships

- **Computer shops:** “Do you occasionally have *owner-released* faulty PCs, discarded components or chargers that are uneconomical for you to repair? Could we discuss a five-device pilot, with clear exclusions and no promises about data erasure?”
- **Employers:** “Who handles retired or surplus office laptops, and do existing finance, ITAD or recycling agreements allow any reuse or community donation? I'm only looking to learn your process at this stage.”
- **Independent schools:** “Would your business manager or ICT coordinator be willing to explain the school's device-disposal policy and whether approved reuse partnerships are possible?”
- **Retailers:** “Do you have a published policy for unwanted demo equipment, and can the store approve community donations, or is that decided centrally?”
- **Repair Café contacts:** Seek a **referral/skills relationship**, not donated customer devices still awaiting pickup.

## Due diligence: what to establish before accepting anything

1. Written confirmation that the person transferring a device is entitled to do so; distinguish business-owned, client-owned, vendor-warranty, leased and trade-in goods.
2. Accurate inventory of serial, model, battery condition, accessories, custody events and acceptance decision.
3. Data-bearing media removed or a documented sanitisation/authorisation process before reuse. Avoid promising certified erasure without verified tooling, procedures and evidence.
4. Appropriate fire-safe handling for swollen/damaged lithium batteries; reject unsafe items and use a lawful specialist pathway.
5. Authorised route for any item ultimately needing recycling; do not scavenge from regulated collection systems.

**Official authority:** [NSW Education device disposal procedure](https://education.nsw.gov.au/policy-library/policies/pd-2024-0481-01); [Australian Government NTCRS](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/protection/waste/product-stewardship/television-computer-recycling-scheme); [ACSC Information Security Manual – media (Sep 2026)](https://www.cyber.gov.au/ism/2026-09/guidelines-for-media).

## Three learning lessons

1. **Find an uncontacted lead (10 min):** Select one repair business and one employer. Explain why their potential streams and decision-makers differ. **Check:** A phone shop doing repairs does not imply it owns customer devices.
2. **Qualify transfer authority (15 min):** Design a five-device hypothetical intake sheet: owner, approval, data status, transport, failures and downstream route. **Check:** A device without an authorised releaser is not a donation.
3. **Make a sustainable outreach plan (10 min):** Choose only two conversations for the coming fortnight; record hours, outcomes and follow-up dates. **Check:** Success is permission and a documented next step, not volume.

## Four-week lightweight follow-through

- **Week 1:** Verify current contact for Custom Computer Creations and ask Inland Technology about its existing equipment pathways. Don't contact Avance clients without appropriate boundaries.
- **Week 2:** If these reply, arrange one 15-minute discovery chat; ask one Westhaven/MAAS/Fletcher contact who owns the retirement decision, no broad mail merge.
- **Week 3:** Write a one-page authorised five-device pilot and check the costs/time and data responsibilities with someone competent.
- **Week 4:** Review responses and hours; stop or change the approach if prospects already have binding disposal contracts or you lack capacity.
