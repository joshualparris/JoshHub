# Inside Dubbo’s technology and repair businesses

Version 2026-10-10.1 · Reviewed 10 October 2026

A public-source learning module covering 15 locations across 14 businesses: operating models, people, workplace evidence and practical reuse partnerships.

## How to use this module

Six lessons, approximately 100 minutes plus optional fieldwork. Complete each practice task and knowledge check, then use the business dossiers as a reference. Full org charts and insider testimony are unavailable for most businesses; public role maps and explicit gaps are provided instead.

## Research method and limits

Research snapshot: 10 October 2026. Scope is 14 businesses operating across the 15 locations in the supplied list; Case Indulgence has two locations. This is public-source research, not access to confidential internal information.

Evidence was sought through business websites, team and career pages, ABN records, vendor directories, local reporting, public professional profiles and employee-review searches. Search themes covered ownership, roles, recruitment, service terms, refurbishment, retirement and workplace conditions. No business or employee was contacted.

Official pages establish what a business publishes, not independent proof of service quality or working conditions. Job advertisements describe a role at a particular time and place. Customer reviews do not establish employee experience; employee reviews are anecdotes with location, age and sample-size limits.

Named roles below are public role maps, not verified reporting charts. Where direct reporting lines, staffing, pay, rosters, tools, throughput or culture are not documented, they remain unknown. National or regional information is labelled and must not be read as Dubbo-specific.

Opportunities, rankings and proposed workflows are research inferences. None establishes willingness to donate, available stock, ownership authority or an agreed partnership. Dates refer to source dates where available; undated sources carry an access date. Stale indexed roles are not current vacancies.

Driving distances and travel times were not reverified; no 20-minute exclusion or distance ranking is asserted. Confirm the Hi Tech street address before visiting. Confirm all appointments, current staff roles and stock arrangements directly.

## Learning pathway

### 1. Understand how the businesses earn their money · 15 minutes

**Objective:** Distinguish a walk-in repair shop, managed IT provider, print dealer and specialist installer.

Start with SOS, Tech Savvy and Case Indulgence for retail diagnosis, repair and resale. Contrast Gravity, Colton and Hi Tech for ongoing support and projects. Read Orana, Viatek and Winc for print/device service, then Desspos for POS installation. The dossiers identify each source and its limits.

A customer journey can be sketched as request → authorisation → diagnosis or scope → work → testing → handover → support. This is a proposed comparison framework, not a claim that all firms use identical workflows.

Ask who pays and for what: labour, parts, fixed project, subscription, device lease or usage. A working device being replaced may still belong to a finance provider or customer. Selling new equipment does not establish control over the old equipment.

**Practice:** Choose one retailer and one MSP. Draw their likely handoffs, then mark every unverified stage with a question mark.

**Check:** A provider replaces a client laptop. What can you conclude?

1. The provider can donate it

2. Ownership and release authority still need checking

3. It must be recycled

**Answer:** 2. Replacement work shows a possible contact point, not title to the outgoing asset.

### 2. Read the people map without inventing an org chart · 15 minutes

**Objective:** Identify a useful functional contact and separate titles from reporting relationships.

Gravity publishes technical, regional and accounts roles. Colton publishes leadership, service delivery, dispatch and sales roles. Hi Tech publishes management, service delivery and project roles. These provide stronger public role maps than the smaller retailers.

A managing director can be a useful introduction, while a service manager may understand actual workflow. A dispatcher may know handoff problems but may not authorise stock release. A published team list is not proof of current local availability.

For a small business, ask who owns the decision. For a larger chain or dealer, ask what the branch can approve and what must go to a national asset, finance or supplier team. Do not infer ownership from a surname or reporting lines from website order.

**Practice:** For three businesses, record one evidenced role, one unknown reporting relationship, and one question that would identify the decision owner.

**Check:** A website lists a service manager and two technicians. Is their reporting line verified?

1. Yes, website order establishes hierarchy

2. Only if job titles sound senior

3. No, the reporting relationship needs evidence

**Answer:** 3. A role map can show published roles without drawing unsupported manager-to-employee arrows.

### 3. Investigate what working there is actually like · 20 minutes

**Objective:** Evaluate employer claims and prepare questions that uncover daily working conditions.

Read CBM’s explicit employment expectations, the Colton regional role and the Hi Tech role comparison. These reveal more about tasks and expectations than generic employer branding, but local pay, current vacancies and current rostering still need confirmation.

Winc national reviews and Viatek older reviews have limited local relevance. Tech Savvy has a local worker interview, but one interview cannot establish an overall culture. For most small shops, public employee evidence was insufficient for a reliable culture verdict.

Ask for a recent example: how did the team handle a difficult ticket, an incorrect diagnosis, a parts delay or an after-hours call? Ask about ticket targets, escalation, paid training time, lone work, travel, weekend cover, overtime arrangements and the first 90 days.

For project compatibility, ask how outside volunteering, repair activities and commercial conflicts are handled. Evaluate the actual written role and agreement; do not assume a friendly interview overrides employment conditions.

**Practice:** Prepare six interview questions and an evidence sheet with columns for claim, example, source/date and what still needs checking.

**Check:** Two old interstate employee reviews are best treated as…

1. Proof of the current Dubbo culture

2. Limited prompts for questions, with date and location stated

3. Evidence of every team member’s experience

**Answer:** 2. Review samples are self-selected; age and local relevance matter.

### 4. Trace a device through repair, reuse and retirement · 20 minutes

**Objective:** Design a small, documented handoff that respects ownership and existing arrangements.

Orana advertises refurbished devices; Tech Savvy sells and repairs pre-owned equipment; Leading Edge lists refurbished stock. These are reasons to discuss complementary work, not assume they will give up saleable stock.

Colton’s published guide separately scopes e-waste disposal. Winc and Viatek already describe lifecycle or recycling pathways. Ask what is already contracted and where there is an unmet need before proposing collection.

Proposed AssetFlow fields: source organisation, authorised transfer reference, asset ID, category, serial where appropriate, condition, accessories, data-bearing status, sanitisation evidence reference, custody events, destination and acceptance. These are design suggestions, not an inventory of any business’s internal software.

A pilot should specify eligible categories, who owns and releases items, data responsibilities, transport, rejection handling, costs, evidence and the final destination. Start with a small agreed lot and review the results before repeating.

**Practice:** Write a one-page handoff for up to five owner-released laptops. Include the point at which you would decline or pause acceptance.

**Check:** An existing recycling arrangement means…

1. All devices are available for free

2. The business cannot discuss reuse

3. You should understand existing obligations and gaps first

**Answer:** 3. A useful partnership complements the actual service and custody arrangements.

### 5. Turn findings into separate project experiments · 15 minutes

**Objective:** Choose a focused next step for each project instead of one generic partnership pitch.

DubboEwaste: validate one released-device stream and one documented handoff. Repair Café: validate a specialist referral or mentoring relationship. Circular Economy Dubbo: document a repeatable local repair/refurbishment pathway.

Library of Things: validate demand, maintainability, accessories and support before accepting equipment. AssetFlow: test the proposed custody and evidence fields using synthetic records; do not copy customer data into a prototype.

Career learning: compare task variety, training, service pressure and after-hours expectations. None of the rankings here is a best-employer award or a promise of a vacancy.

A discovery conversation should end with a small question resolved: who decides, what categories exist, what conditions apply, or whether there is any fit. A clear “no” is useful evidence.

**Practice:** Select two businesses and two different project goals. Write a specific 15-minute conversation agenda for each.

**Check:** What is the strongest initial project outcome?

1. A verified decision owner and a scoped next step

2. A large stock-volume estimate based on store size

3. One pitch sent to every business

**Answer:** 1. Resolving a concrete uncertainty is more useful than premature scale assumptions.

### 6. Run a 30-day learning cycle · 15 minutes

**Objective:** Convert research into a small set of observed, reviewable decisions.

Week 1: read three priority dossiers, verify current contact roles and record five unanswered questions. Build a synthetic asset handoff and a separate workplace interview sheet.

Week 2: if you choose to initiate outreach, request two short discovery conversations. State the project and ask about existing arrangements. No outreach was sent as part of this research.

Week 3: document answers, distinguish quotes from interpretation, and design one optional pilot with written scope. Do not accept stock or make service promises before the relevant authority and capacity are established.

Week 4: review evidence, time spent, declined items, verified transfers and useful referrals. Update the source record and decide to stop, adjust or repeat. Measure useful outcomes rather than the number of businesses contacted.

**Practice:** Complete a plan with two priorities, one decision owner to verify, one measurable outcome and one stop condition.

**Check:** Which finding should cause the source record to be updated?

1. A direct confirmation that a listed manager has left

2. An assumption based on the size of the shop

3. A customer star rating without workplace evidence

**Answer:** 1. Dated, attributable corrections improve the research and prevent stale role maps.
